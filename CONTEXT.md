# Operating Systems Practical Work Context

This repository contains guided practical exercises for an operating-systems course. The documents help students learn how different parts of an operating system work and how to interact with them through real programs, tools, and observable system behavior.

## Language

**TP (Trabajo Práctico)**:
A guided exercise that teaches students about a part of the operating system and gives them practice interacting with it. The TP series builds understanding by connecting concepts to concrete experiments and implementations.

**Regular material**:
The required, guided part of a TP. It provides the structure and hand-holding students need to learn the core concepts and complete the assignment.

**Inline EXTRA**:
A small optional extension placed inside regular TP material. It adds a question, comparison, or experiment without turning the exercise into a separate research track.

**Extra TP (`-extra`)**:
An optional companion document for students who finish the regular assignment early and want a deeper challenge. It is organized into tracks, gives significantly less hand-holding, and proposes directions for investigation rather than prescribing every step.

**Track**:
A mostly self-contained line of investigation within an extra TP. A good track concerns something a software engineer could use on a daily or weekly basis, exposes students to something they may not already know, and is ideally interesting or “cool” enough to reward curiosity. A track may assume all regular material from its TP and earlier TPs, but no other extra track should be a prerequisite; useful references between tracks are allowed.

**Bonus points task**:
An optional extension within a track that goes substantially beyond the regular extra. It should be difficult for a first-year undergraduate and require an implementation that gives the student deeper understanding and appreciation of the surrounding software ecosystem.

There is no fixed number of bonus tasks. Roughly two tracks with bonus tasks per TP is a useful target, but the quality and fit of the tracks matter more than meeting that number.

**Bonus submission**:
A short document submitted for a bonus points task. Formatting is unimportant; it should briefly explain the work and include screenshots. The submission is corrected manually. Only the first five students who correctly complete a given bonus task are eligible for its points, and instructors may request a live demonstration and related questions to verify understanding and authorship.
